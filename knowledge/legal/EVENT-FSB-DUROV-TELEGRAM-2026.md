# Russia FSB Charges Against Pavel Durov / Telegram (July 2026)

## Summary

On July 29, 2026, Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) charged Telegram founder Pavel Durov with facilitating terrorist activity, alleging that Telegram failed to remove channels, chats, and bots used to prepare and coordinate sabotage and terrorist acts inside Russia, and stated that Durov was being placed on an international wanted list.

## Verified Facts

- Associated Press reported that Russian authorities charged Pavel Durov, founder and CEO of Telegram, with aiding terrorism.[^1]
- AP reported that the FSB accused Telegram's administration of failing to remove "numerous channels, chats and bots" allegedly used by "Ukrainian intelligence agencies, terrorist and extremist organizations to prepare and coordinate acts of sabotage and terrorism, mass murder, and cyber fraud" in Russia.[^1]
- BBC News reported that the FSB said Telegram had been used "to prepare and co-ordinate acts of sabotage and terror" inside Russia and had failed to remove channels, chats, and bots used by Ukraine, and that an international arrest warrant had been issued for Durov.[^2]
- Meduza, citing Interfax, reported that the FSB charged Durov with facilitating terrorist activity under Part 1.1 of Article 205.1 of Russia's Criminal Code and that Durov "is being placed on an international wanted list."[^3]
- AP reported that if convicted, Durov could face up to life in prison in Russia.[^1]
- AP reported that Durov had said earlier in 2026 that Russian authorities opened a criminal investigation against him and accused them of fabricating pretexts to restrict Telegram and suppress privacy and free speech.[^1]
- Meduza reported that Durov wrote in late February 2026 that Russia had opened a criminal case against him for facilitating terrorism and characterized the case as a pretext to restrict Telegram.[^3]
- AP reported that Telegram did not respond to a request for comment on the July 29, 2026 charges.[^1]

## Historical Context

These charges arise amid broader Russian restrictions on Telegram and other foreign platforms after the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. They also follow French criminal proceedings against Durov in 2024–2026 over alleged platform moderation failures. OIR records the FSB's accusations as allegations reported by secondary sources; they are not treated here as adjudicated findings that Telegram supports terrorism.

## Legal Analysis

OIR has not yet located the FSB primary statement, charging instrument, or full Criminal Code text as applied. Comparative analysis should separate (1) state allegations about third-party channel use, (2) platform-operator mens rea or knowledge theories, and (3) wartime information-control incentives.

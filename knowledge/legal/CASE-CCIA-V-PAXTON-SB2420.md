# CCIA v. Paxton (Texas SB 2420 / App Store Accountability Act)

## Summary

*Computer & Communications Industry Association v. Paxton* (W.D. Tex. No. 1:25-CV-1660-RP), together with a parallel challenge by Students Engaged in Advancing Texas, contests Texas SB 2420 (the App Store Accountability Act) on First Amendment and related grounds. Judge Robert Pitman granted a preliminary injunction on December 23, 2025. The Fifth Circuit administratively stayed the injunctions on May 28, 2026, then stayed them pending appeal on June 4, 2026. On July 6, 2026, the Supreme Court denied both emergency applications to vacate that stay (Nos. 25A1390 and 25A1389). The statute remains enforceable while the merits appeal continues; there is no final appellate holding on constitutionality as of last verification.

## Verified Facts

- Plaintiffs challenge Texas SB 2420’s app-store age verification, parental-consent, and developer age-rating regime.[^1][^4]
- On December 23, 2025, the Western District of Texas (Judge Pitman) granted a preliminary injunction against enforcement, finding the Act likely unconstitutional.[^2][^4]
- The companion district case is *Students Engaged in Advancing Texas; M. F., by and through next friend Vanessa Fernandez; and Z. B., by and through next friend S.B. v. Paxton*, W.D. Tex. No. 1:25-CV-1662. The Fifth Circuit consolidated it with CCIA’s appeal as No. 25-51073 c/w No. 26-50001.[^3]
- On May 28, 2026, the Fifth Circuit filed an unpublished order temporarily and administratively staying the preliminary injunctions while Texas’s motion for a stay pending appeal was still in briefing. The June 4, 2026 published stay says that it supersedes that administrative stay.[^3][^10]
- On June 4, 2026, the Fifth Circuit (Judges Smith, Haynes, and Oldham) stayed the preliminary injunctions pending appeal. Judge Haynes concurred in the sentence of the order granting the stay. The stay allows enforcement during the appeal.[^3]
- On June 10, 2026, CCIA and the SEAT applicants each submitted an application to vacate that stay to Justice Alito. The applications were docketed June 15, 2026, as Nos. 25A1390 and 25A1389.[^7][^9][^11]
- On July 6, 2026, the Supreme Court denied No. 25A1390: the application to vacate the stay, presented to Justice Alito and by him referred to the Court, is denied.[^6][^7]
- The same day the Court denied the SEAT applicants’ companion application, No. 25A1389, in the same terms.[^8][^9]
- Counsel of record on the Supreme Court applications are Brian Michael Willen (Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati) for CCIA, Ambika Kumar (Davis Wright Tremaine) for the SEAT applicants, and William Robert Peterson (Office of the Texas Attorney General) for the respondent on both applications.[^7][^9]
- CCIA’s emergency application identifies the Fifth Circuit CCIA appeal as No. 26-50001. The Supreme Court docket header for No. 25A1390 instead prints the lower-court case number as (25-50001). The Fifth Circuit stay order and opinion PDF use No. 26-50001.[^3][^7][^11]
- The Fifth Circuit’s August 4, 2026 New Orleans sitting calendar listed No. 25-51073, consolidated with No. 26-50001, for 9:00 a.m. argument in the East Courtroom before Judges Stewart, Graves, and Wilson.[^12]
- The Texas Tribune article at the May 28, 2026 URL was updated July 6, 2026. The updated text reports that the Supreme Court declined to block enforcement while the Fifth Circuit appeal continues, and that the court of appeals had allowed enforcement in late May.[^5]

## Legal Analysis

The district court analogized SB 2420 to requiring every bookstore customer to verify age and minors to obtain parental consent for entry and purchases—an overbreadth/speech-restriction framing under the First Amendment.[^2] The Fifth Circuit stay is an interim appellate posture, not a merits affirmance. The July 6 denials are one-line order-list entries, not a ruling on constitutionality. Pair with [STAT-TX-APP-STORE-ACCOUNTABILITY](STAT-TX-APP-STORE-ACCOUNTABILITY.md), [TOPIC-APP-STORE-AGE-VERIFICATION](TOPIC-APP-STORE-AGE-VERIFICATION.md), and [TOPIC-FIRST-AMENDMENT](TOPIC-FIRST-AMENDMENT.md).

The June 4 stay order cites *Free Speech Coalition, Inc. v. Paxton*, 606 U.S. 461, on intermediate-scrutiny tailoring.[^3] That is a website age-verification precedent. OIR does not yet have a case page for it.

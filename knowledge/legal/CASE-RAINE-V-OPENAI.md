# Raine v. OpenAI, Inc.

## Summary

Raine v. OpenAI, Inc., No. CGC-25-628528 (Cal. Super. Ct., S.F. Cnty.), is a pending wrongful-death and product-liability action filed August 26, 2025, by Matthew and Maria Raine over the death of their 16-year-old son Adam. The original complaint names OpenAI entities and Samuel Altman and pleads design-defect, failure-to-warn, UCL, wrongful-death, and survival theories. The case was later coordinated into [In re ChatGPT Product Liability Cases](CASE-IN-RE-CHATGPT-PRODUCT-LIABILITY.md). The sources reviewed here contain no merits ruling.

## Verified Facts

- On August 26, 2025, Matthew and Maria Raine, individually and as successors-in-interest to decedent Adam Raine, filed a San Francisco Superior Court complaint against OpenAI, Inc., OpenAI OpCo, LLC, OpenAI Holdings, LLC, Samuel Altman, and unnamed employees and investors.[^1]
- Plaintiffs allege that Adam Raine, then 16 and a California resident, began using ChatGPT in September 2024 and died by suicide on April 11, 2025.[^1]
- The complaint alleges that GPT-4o was designed with persistent memory, anthropomorphic mannerisms, and sycophantic engagement that fostered dependency and that OpenAI launched those features despite internal safety objections.[^1]
- The complaint pleads seven causes of action: (1) strict product liability — design defect; (2) strict product liability — failure to warn; (3) negligence — design defect; (4) negligence — failure to warn; (5) violation of California’s Unfair Competition Law; (6) wrongful death; and (7) a survival action.[^1]
- Florida’s later Attorney General complaint cites this action as Raine v. OpenAI, No. CGC-25-628528 (Cal. Super. Ct. Aug. 26, 2025).[^2]
- Counsel in the California ChatGPT product-liability cluster reported that the Judicial Council coordinated those actions, including GPT-4o design claims of the kind pleaded here, as JCCP No. 5431 on February 3, 2026.[^3]

## Historical Context

Raine is the first widely filed U.S. wrongful-death suit against OpenAI over an alleged chatbot-related suicide. It supplied the pleading template later recycled across the California coordination and cited by Florida’s Attorney General. [Garcia](CASE-GARCIA-V-CHARACTER-TECHNOLOGIES.md) had already tested companion-chatbot product liability in federal court; Raine brought the same cluster of theories against a general-purpose model and its CEO.

## Legal Analysis

Jurisdiction: California Superior Court, later coordinated in San Francisco. Authority level: complaint and coordination reporting. No court in the sources reviewed here has decided causation, whether ChatGPT is a product, or whether the First Amendment or Section 230 bars the claims.[^1][^3]

Naming Altman individually is a pleading choice also used in later ChatGPT harm suits. Treat it as an allegation of personal participation in design and launch decisions, not a finding of personal liability.

This page does not state that any defendant is liable.

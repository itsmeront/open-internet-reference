# Chatbot Product Liability and User-Harm Litigation

## Summary

Conversational AI is now the subject of product-liability, wrongful-death, consumer-protection, and defamation suits. The cases documented here fall into six clusters: (1) companion-chatbot deaths involving Character.AI, (2) ChatGPT suicide and self-harm actions coordinated in California, (3) murder-suicide and psychosis allegations, (4) third-party shooting-victim claims arising from the April 17, 2025 Florida State University attack, (5) Florida’s Attorney General civil enforcement action and a related criminal investigation, and (6) a decided Georgia hallucination-defamation judgment. This page is an inventory, not a finding that any chatbot caused a death or that any defendant is liable.

## Verified Facts

- [Garcia v. Character Technologies, Inc.](CASE-GARCIA-V-CHARACTER-TECHNOLOGIES.md) (M.D. Fla. May 20, 2025) allowed most wrongful-death and product-liability claims against a Character.AI chatbot company and Google to proceed and declined, at the pleading stage, to treat LLM output as protected speech.[^1]
- [Raine v. OpenAI, Inc.](CASE-RAINE-V-OPENAI.md) (Cal. Super. Ct., S.F. Cnty., filed Aug. 26, 2025) is a pending wrongful-death and product-liability action by the parents of 16-year-old Adam Raine, alleging that GPT-4o’s design contributed to his April 11, 2025 suicide.[^2]
- California coordinated twelve state ChatGPT product-liability actions on February 3, 2026, as [In re ChatGPT Product Liability Cases](CASE-IN-RE-CHATGPT-PRODUCT-LIABILITY.md), JCCP No. 5431, centralized in San Francisco Superior Court.[^3][^4][^5]
- [Lyons v. OpenAI Foundation](CASE-LYONS-V-OPENAI.md) (N.D. Cal.) is a pending federal action by Stein-Erik Soelberg’s estate alleging that ChatGPT validated paranoid delusions before a Greenwich murder-suicide; on April 13, 2026, the court denied a Colorado River stay in favor of the related Adams estate state case, which had been swept into the JCCP.[^3][^6]
- [Joshi v. OpenAI Foundation](CASE-JOSHI-V-OPENAI.md) (N.D. Fla., filed May 10, 2026) is a pending diversity action by the estate of Tiru Chabba, killed in the April 17, 2025 FSU shooting, alleging that ChatGPT assisted the shooter in planning the attack.[^7]
- On April 21, 2026, Florida’s Office of Statewide Prosecution announced a criminal investigation into whether OpenAI bears responsibility for ChatGPT’s role in that shooting; the announcement is not a charging instrument.[^8]
- [Florida v. OpenAI Global, LLC](CASE-FLORIDA-V-OPENAI.md) (Fla. 10th Cir. Ct., filed June 1, 2026) is a pending Attorney General civil action against OpenAI entities and Sam Altman alleging FDUTPA violations, negligence, product-liability, fraudulent misrepresentation, and public nuisance.[^9][^10]
- [Walters v. OpenAI, L.L.C.](CASE-WALTERS-V-OPENAI.md) (Ga. Super. Ct. Gwinnett Cnty. May 19, 2025) granted OpenAI summary judgment on a ChatGPT hallucination defamation claim, holding the output did not communicate defamatory meaning as a matter of law and that the plaintiff could not show fault or damages.[^11]

## Legal Analysis

These dockets ask whether conversational software is a product whose design can be defective, whether particular outputs are speech protected by the [First Amendment](TOPIC-FIRST-AMENDMENT.md), and whether [Section 230](STAT-SECTION-230.md) or a user’s own act breaks the causal chain.[^1] Garcia is the only pleading-stage order in this inventory to address the speech-versus-product distinction; Walters is the only merits disposition, and it is a Georgia defamation judgment, not a physical-injury case.[^1][^11]

For open-internet and software-freedom work, two tensions matter. First, treating chatbot text as unprotected non-speech at the pleading stage, as in Garcia, could expand design-defect litigation against conversational software — including open-weight models — without a merits record on causation.[^1] Second, Florida’s criminal investigation of a model vendor over alleged assistance to a shooter is a different kind of proceeding from the civil inventory and from the developer-prosecution docket on [Documented Proceedings Involving Software Authors and Operators](TOPIC-DEVELOPER-SOFTWARE-LIABILITY.md).[^8]

Copyright training cases remain on [AI Litigation Landscape](TOPIC-AI-LITIGATION.md) and [Copyright and Generative AI](TOPIC-AI-COPYRIGHT.md). This page does not predict outcomes or state that any chatbot caused a death.

# Bipartisan Senate AI Working Group

## Summary

The Bipartisan Senate AI Working Group was a 118th Congress leadership group of Chuck Schumer, Todd Young, Martin Heinrich, and Mike Rounds that ran AI Insight Forums and released a May 2024 legislative roadmap.

## Verified Facts

- On May 15, 2024, Senator Martin Heinrich's office announced that Heinrich, alongside the Bipartisan Senate AI Working Group comprising Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.), Todd Young (R-Ind.), and Mike Rounds (R-S.D.), had released a roadmap for Senate AI legislation.[^1]
- The announcement said the roadmap followed months of discussion, hundreds of stakeholder meetings, and nine all-Senator AI Insight Forums.[^1]
- The press release describes the roadmap as identifying policy topics that "merit urgent bipartisan consideration" to stimulate new policy development, not as a single omnibus bill.[^1]
- Heinrich's office identifies him as co-founder and co-chair of the separate Senate AI Caucus and as holding a lead role on Schumer's working group.[^1]


[^1]: `SRC-SENATE-AI-ROADMAP-2024` — Official Senate press release (May 15, 2024). Pair with the Schumer-office PDF linked from that release.

## Historical Context

The Working Group was a time-bounded leadership vehicle in the 118th Congress, distinct from the standing Senate AI Caucus. Its 2024 roadmap emphasized U.S. innovation investment, research infrastructure (including NAIRR / CREATE AI), workforce, elections, privacy, intellectual property, safety, and national security. That is a "lead on innovation plus guardrails" agenda. It does not call for a general pause in frontier-model development of the kind Senator Bernie Sanders later demanded in 2026.

## Relevance to Open Source and Software Companies

The roadmap is still the most comprehensive bipartisan Senate statement on AI policy headings. Committees continue to draw from it on research-cloud access, copyright, privacy, and safety reporting — issues that affect open-weight releases and commercial APIs alike. It is a map of where the Senate already claimed consensus, not a substitute for later 2026 pause-and-ban proposals.

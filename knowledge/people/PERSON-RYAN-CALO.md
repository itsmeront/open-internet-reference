# Ryan Calo

## Summary

Ryan Calo is the Virginia and Prentice Bloedel Professor at the University of Washington, a co-founder of the UW Tech Policy Lab, and a leading academic on law and artificial intelligence who has testified before the U.S. Senate.

## Verified Facts

- UW Law lists Calo as Virginia and Prentice Bloedel Professor at the University of Washington, Professor of Law, Professor in the Information School, and adjunct professor in the Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science and Engineering.[^1]
- He is a founding co-director of the interdisciplinary UW Tech Policy Lab and a co-founder of the UW Center for an Informed Public.[^1]
- His listed research areas include law and emerging technology, privacy, artificial intelligence, and robotics.[^1]
- UW Law states that he has testified four times before the United States Senate, most recently on July 11, 2024, before the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation at a hearing titled "The Need to Protect Americans' Privacy and the AI Accelerant."[^1]
- At that 2024 hearing, the faculty page says he stressed the importance of a comprehensive federal privacy law that both protects personal privacy and sets guidelines for businesses developing and implementing AI technology.[^1]


[^1]: `SRC-CALO-UW` — Official University of Washington School of Law faculty page.

## Expert Testimony and Public Advocacy

Calo is a first-stop academic for "what does AI law even cover?" — robotics, automated decision-making, privacy, and how existing doctrines strain when software acts with apparent autonomy. His Senate record makes him a natural counterpart to oversight letters such as Blumenthal's after the Hugging Face incident. Treat him as a scholar and witness, not as retained litigation counsel, unless a specific appearance is sourced.

## Relevance to Lawsuits Involving Software and Internet Infrastructure

Calo is particularly useful when a matter involves:

- Whether an autonomous agent’s actions fit existing tort, privacy, or computer-crime categories
- Federal privacy legislation as a vehicle for AI rules
- Explaining robotics and cyberlaw analogies to judges

# In re ChatGPT Product Liability Cases

## Summary

In re ChatGPT Product Liability Cases, JCCP No. 5431 (Cal. Super. Ct., S.F. Cnty.), is the California coordinated proceeding for state-court product-liability and wrongful-death actions against OpenAI over ChatGPT. A February 3, 2026 coordination granted a petition covering twelve state actions, including the Adams estate suit related to [Lyons v. OpenAI Foundation](CASE-LYONS-V-OPENAI.md). The coordination is a pretrial-management device, not a finding of liability.

## Verified Facts

- On February 3, 2026, a California court granted a petition coordinating twelve state actions against ChatGPT that allege product-liability theories, including the Adams estate action filed December 11, 2025.[^1]
- Plaintiffs’ counsel reported the coordinated caption as ChatGPT Product Liability Cases, JCCP No. 5431, centralized in San Francisco Superior Court, covering actions from Los Angeles, San Francisco, Alameda, and San Diego and presenting common questions about whether GPT-4o is a defective product.[^2]
- Secondary legal journalism describes the same proceeding as In re ChatGPT Product Liability Cases, JCCP No. 5431, grouping claims that ChatGPT is unreasonably dangerous because of alleged sycophantic design, reinforcement of delusions or suicidal ideation, and inadequate safety testing.[^3]
- [Raine v. OpenAI, Inc.](CASE-RAINE-V-OPENAI.md), filed August 26, 2025, in San Francisco Superior Court, is the earliest widely circulated complaint in that California cluster.[^4]
- The federal Lyons action was not absorbed into the JCCP; the Northern District of California declined to stay Lyons in favor of the coordinated Adams state case.[^1]

## Historical Context

JCCP 5431 is the state-court counterpart to copyright’s [In re OpenAI, Inc., Copyright Infringement Litigation](CASE-IN-RE-OPENAI.md): a management vehicle for many similar complaints, not a single class judgment. Federal FSU and Soelberg-estate cases remain outside it.

## Legal Analysis

Jurisdiction: California Superior Court, San Francisco, sitting as coordination court. Authority level: coordination grant as recited by a federal court and counsel reporting. The sources reviewed here do not include a merits ruling on product defect, speech, or Section 230.[^1][^2]

Coordination does not merge the plaintiffs’ damages claims. Each constituent action still has to prove causation for that user or decedent. OpenAI’s characterization of ChatGPT as a service rather than a product, noted in secondary commentary, remains an unresolved defense.[^3]
